02 a promise with a number on it

said: 3 lines · took: 3 lines · ×1.0
saidan estimate written down before the first commit: in your tracker, in the commit, or in the ledger
first commit → merge, in working hours, in the time zone your own commits carry
×took ÷ said for every task you finished, and the median of those, taken in log space

The auth migration took 9.6 days and shipped on a Friday. The search index took 14 hours. Hover a number — it tells you what was promised.

Your tracker already holds years of estimates (Jira, Linear, GitHub, GitLab), so the first run has history to work with. Without a tracker, put Estimate: 2d in the first commit of a branch. Without either, fibo say PROJ-412 2d writes it down before you start.

03 the multiplier

said: 1 screen · took: 2 screens · ×2.0
0.0×this number is the whole product.
it does not tell you to estimate better.
it tells you what to multiply by.
drift · 90-day rolling mediantwo years → now
3.7×4.5× · last 90 days
median in log space. ×4 and ×¼ are the same distance from ×1, and the median does not care about the one ×40 disaster.
working hours. mon–fri, 9–18, in the offset your own commits carried. a day is 8h, a week is 40h. --calendar takes both sides literally, shown small.
span and active. span: first commit → merge. active clips any pause over three working days. the gap is how long tasks sat waiting.
p90 is the bad day: in the worst case, ×8.6.
by kind of work. tracker labels, then where the diff went (migrations/, tests/), then the words in the title. five tasks to be shown.
fibo PROJ-412 takes one task apart. fibo drift shows it quarter by quarter. fibo doctor checks that nobody nudged anything.

04 what it refuses

said: 4 examples · took: 5 · ×1.25
fibo say PROJ-412 soon✗ not a duration
fibo say PROJ-412 2d✗ written after the work began
a task with no estimate✗ not counted, and not guessed
--whose someone@else.com✗ this tool has one subject
story points, no rate given✗ points are not time

not a duration. "soon", "by the end of the sprint", "quick" are not estimates. say a number and a unit.

written after the work began. the program knows when the field changed and when the first commit was made. the value that stood at the first commit counts. doctor lists the rest.

not guessed. a task without an estimate gets nothing by analogy, by average or by diff size. it is left out, and the output says so.

one subject. yours. it will not print another person's multiplier, aggregate a team, or export. --whose with anyone else's address fails loudly.

points are not time. --points-as-hours=N exists, and the output then says the number rests on your assumption.

That is all of it. No database, no index, no daemon, no server, no account, no telemetry. Every run recomputes from history. With a tracker it only reads — GET and GraphQL queries; a mutation is refused before it is sent.

07 bugs · honest limits

said: 3 · took: 5 · ×1.7
IT CANNOT SEE THINKINGthe clock starts at the first commit, not the first thought. your real multiplier is higher than the one printed.
IT CANNOT SEE INTERRUPTIONa branch that sat for a week looks like a week of work. active is there for that, and it is still an approximation.
THE DEMO IS A DEMOreproducible, and nobody's record of promises. yours starts empty.
IT DOES NOT KNOW THE RIGHT ANSWERthere is no correct estimate. it knows what you said and how long it took.
IT PREDICTS NOTHINGno model, no opinion. what was written down, what happened, one division.

08 roadmap · the ledger

said: 7 lines · took: 0 closed · ×—

Promises with numbers on them, in the program's own units: a day is 8 h, a week is 40 h. When a line ships, its took is written next to its said, and the multiplier that comes out is what the rest of this list should be multiplied by.

Order, not calendar. A said is a length, not a date, and nothing here moves up because someone asked.
